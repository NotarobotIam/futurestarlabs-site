      06 · Experiment

      Hold one end cold. Heat the other.

      Every charge carrier jiggles with temperature, but only a difference in temperature makes them drift. That drift is a voltage you can measure.

      ΔT
      +300°C
      Output voltage
      12.30mV
      V = α · ΔT, α ≈ 41 µV/°C (Type K)

      A linear approximation. A real Type K junction's response bends slightly with temperature, so lab work uses reference tables or polynomials.

      The problem

      Biology is still hard to automate.

      Manual handling

      Biological experiments depend on hands-on sample preparation that is slow and hard to standardise.

      Reproducibility

      Results shift between operators, runs and labs, so the same test can give different answers.

      Cost and training

      Consumables are expensive, and every assay takes skilled staff time to learn and run well.

      Machines are learning the bench

      AI-driven robots are learning to pipette, handle samples and run experiments. They need assays built for machines: consistent, measurable and controlled by software. Future Star Labs builds its assays that way from the start.

      Our approach

      Engineer the assay itself.

      Instead of bolting robots onto existing lab equipment, we design the assay so it can be automated from the start. We control where cells sit and what they are exposed to, then connect that to sensing and software.

      1. 01

        Engineered surfaces

        Separate and position cells so every run starts the same way.

        Demonstrated
      2. 02

        Miniaturised fluidics

        A lab-on-a-disc format that moves samples and reagents. Three design iterations completed so far.

        In development
      3. 03

        Impedance sensing

        An electrical readout of how cells respond, with no stains or manual counting.

        Demonstrated
      4. 04

        Automated workflows

        Python instrument control, signal processing and data analysis.

        In development

        Physical AI integration

        Software-defined setup, measurement and AI-assisted interpretation of impedance signals, through a web interface and lab automation systems.

      BacteSign

      An engineered assay for bacterial identification, antibiotic susceptibility testing and antimicrobial resistance research, designed to plug into automated and AI-driven labs.

      Bacterial proof of conceptResearch-use system in development
      01

      Identify

      Separate and position bacteria on an engineered surface so they can be told apart.

      02

      Test susceptibility

      Expose positioned bacteria to an antibiotic and watch whether they keep growing.

      03

      Study resistance

      Compare how strains respond across antibiotics and concentrations for AMR research.

      How a run works

      Five steps, one readout.

      1. 01

        Load the sample

        The sample goes onto the assay disc.

      2. 02

        Separate and position

        Engineered surfaces hold bacteria in defined spots.

      3. 03

        Expose

        Each spot meets an antibiotic at a set concentration, next to an untreated control.

      4. 04

        Measure impedance

        As bacteria grow and metabolise, the electrical impedance of their surroundings drops. Electrodes track that change over time.

      5. 05

        Analyse

        Comparing treated and control signals shows whether the antibiotic stopped growth.
